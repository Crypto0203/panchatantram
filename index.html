🧠 Child Psychology & Retention Engineering

The neuroscience of short-form video for kids: Why they swipe, how to hold attention, and how to command automatic follows.

1. The 2.5-Second Swipe Reflex

Children and parents scrolling social media have an average attention threshold of 2 seconds. The brain requires immediate visual contrast or dramatic danger to inhibit the thumb swipe.

Rule: Never begin on a wide landscape or static title card. Begin directly on high-speed motion, an animal's shocked eyes, or a host gasp.

2. The 4-Part Binge-Follow Engine

To make viewers follow automatically, the final 4 seconds must resolve one emotional beat while immediately opening a brand-new curiosity loop for tomorrow.

  • • Pill Banner: Part X of 100 (completionist trigger).
  • • 1-Line Moral: Emotional warmth.
  • • 1.5s Sneak Peek: Unanswered cliffhanger question.
  • • Bouncing Follow Sticker: Direct visual + audio command.

3. The Micro-SFX Checklist (Audio is 60%)

Silent or dialogue-heavy videos fail with young children. Every 30-second episode must have at least 6 to 8 cartoony sound effects.

0:01s Gasp/Boing 0:03s Magic Chime 0:07s Animal Steps 0:12s Danger Sting 0:21s Splash/Thud 0:25s Fanfare 0:29s Bell Ding
